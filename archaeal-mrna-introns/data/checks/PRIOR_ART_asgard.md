# Prior art: introns in archaeal protein-coding genes (subagent a2b8590ab815ffa80, report saved verbatim by Vega, 13 Nov 23:54)

Brief: are introns in archaeal protein genes known; in Asgard; in RuBisCO-like or pilin/archaellin genes; recent surveys. The agent did not see my counts.

---

I found no report of an intron in any Asgard protein-coding gene, and none in any archaeal RuBisCO-like protein or pilin/archaellin gene. The only intron in an archaeal protein-coding gene that has been shown experimentally is the one in cbf5, in Crenarchaeota. A few other cases come from RNA-seq read patterns alone. I went through the full texts of the key papers myself.

**Q1. Introns in archaeal protein-coding genes**

1. **cbf5 (pseudouridine synthase) in *Aeropyrum pernix*, *Sulfolobus solfataricus* and *S. tokodaii*: shown by RT-PCR.** Watanabe Y, Yokobori S, Inaba T, Yamagishi A, Oshima T, Kawarabayasi Y, Kikuchi H, Kita K. 2002. FEBS Lett 510:27–30. doi:10.1016/S0014-5793(01)03219-7. The abstract says "Splicing of pre-mRNAs in vivo was demonstrated by reverse transcriptase-mediated polymerase chain reaction", and the boundaries fold into a structure "similar to the bulge-helix-bulge motif".
2. **cbf5 in *S. tokodaii*: the protein and the cleavage were shown.** Yoshinari S, Itoh T, Hallam SJ, DeLong EF, Yokobori S, Yamagishi A, Oshima T, Kita K, Watanabe Y. 2006. Biochem Biophys Res Commun 346:1024–1032. doi:10.1016/j.bbrc.2006.06.011. They report "immunological detection of Cbf5 protein of S. tokodaii, the product of the spliced cbf5 mRNA". The recombinant splicing endonuclease cut the pre-mRNA at the exon-intron boundaries. They also found further cbf5 introns, some with "relaxed forms of the bulge-helix-bulge motif".
3. **A survey of cbf5 across Crenarchaeota.** Yokobori S, Itoh T, Yoshinari S, Nomura N, Sako Y, Yamagishi A, Oshima T, Kita K, Watanabe Y. 2009. BMC Evol Biol 9:198. doi:10.1186/1471-2148-9-198.
   - Desulfurococcales with the intron (16–44 bp): for example *A. pernix* 38, *Desulfurococcus mobilis* 16, *Thermodiscus maritimus* 44.
   - Sulfolobales with the intron (19–31 bp): for example *S. solfataricus* 22, *S. tokodaii* 31, *Metallosphaera sedula* 19.
   - No intron: Pyrodictiaceae, *Ignicoccus*, *Pyrobaculum*, *Thermofilum*, Thaumarchaeota and *Korarchaeum*.
   - The intron "existed in the common ancestor of the orders Desulfurococcales and Sulfolobales".
   - Most of these introns come from genome or PCR sequence plus fold prediction. Spliced cDNA for two *Desulfurococcus* species and two other species is reported only as "unpublished results".
4. **RNA-seq circles inside genes in *S. solfataricus*: shown not to be introns.** Danan M, Schwartz S, Edelheit S, Sorek R. 2012. Nucleic Acids Res 40:3131–3142. doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1009. Five circular RNAs lie inside protein-coding genes, but "we failed to detect spliced reads that support a mature form of the mRNA after intron excision". cbf5 showed "seven different circularization junctions" next to the known intron.
5. **Intron candidates inside genes in *Methanopyrus kandleri*: RNA-seq read patterns only.** Berkemer SJ, Höner zu Siederdissen C, Amman F, Wintsche A, Will S, Hofacker IL, Prohaska SJ, Stadler PF. 2015. Genomics Comput Biol 1:e18. https://d-nb.info/1239738552/34
   - "11 circularization products derive from introns within ORFs. Of these, 7 preserve the reading frame."
   - The authors also say their data provide "evidence also for other BHB-dependent introns in coding regions".
   - The genes are listed in their Supplementary Table 2, which I did not open.
6. ***Nanoarchaeum equitans*: box C/D RNAs that overlap protein genes.** Randau L. 2012. Genome Biol 13:R63. doi:10.1186/gb-2012-13-7-r63. Randau writes only that "Most C/D box sRNAs overlap with mRNA sequences", including putative rRNA methyltransferase genes. Calling these mRNA introns is Berkemer et al. 2015's reading, not Randau's.
7. ***Haloferax volcanii* HVO_1309 (M24 peptidase): an intron in the 5′ UTR, not in the coding sequence.** Schwarz TS, Berkemer SJ, Bernhart SH, Weiß M, Ferreira-Cerca S, Stadler PF, Marchfelder A. 2020. Front Microbiol 11:594838. doi:10.3389/fmicb.2020.594838. "Splicing removes 46 nucleotides from the 192 nucleotide long 5′ UTR." The evidence is a circular intron in circRNA-seq plus cleavage in vitro; the spliced mRNA itself was not shown.
8. **Recent reviews still name only cbf5.** Liang Y, Qi W, Dong X, Li J. 2025. Microbiol Mol Biol Rev 89:e00318-24. doi:10.1128/mmbr.00318-24. "Their mRNAs are generally intron-less"; the endonuclease removes introns from "Crenarchaeota cbf5 pre-mRNA".

I found no protein-coding intron reported for *Thermococcus* or *Pyrococcus*; their RNA-seq papers deal with tRNA, rRNA and C/D box RNA circles. A *Halobacterium salinarum* circRNA survey found "one circRNA in a hypothetical protein-coding gene" but does not call it an intron (Picinato BA et al. 2025, Front Microbiol, doi:10.3389/fmicb.2025.1641342).

Confidence: high for items 1–4 and 7. Item 5 has only one supporting source. The question's "Pyrobaculum" lead turned up nothing.

**Q2. Asgard archaea**

I found nothing.
- I searched the full texts of Imachi et al. 2020 (Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-019-1916-6, PMC7015854) and Rodrigues-Oliveira et al. 2023 (Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-022-05550-y, PMC9834061). Neither has any hit for intron, splicing, RuBisCO, pilin, pili or archaellum.
- The 2024 naming paper for *Promethearchaeum* (Int J Syst Evol Microbiol, doi:10.1099/ijsem.0.006435) also has no hits.
- Both cultures do have RNA-seq data:
  - MK-D1: BioProject PRJDB9032, run DRR199588.
  - B35: BioProject PRJNA1054498, from the hydrogenase paper (Cell 2024, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2024.05.032). That paper only notes MAGs encoding "RuBisCO lineages known to function in nucleoside salvage".
  - B35: also a time-series metatranscriptome in "Microtubules in Asgard archaea" (Cell 2025).
- None of these papers mentions spliced mRNAs. I did not re-analyse the reads or check the supplementary tables.
- Shi L-D et al. 2026 (Genome Biol, doi:10.1186/s13059-025-03903-0) report circular 16S and 23S rRNA in Asgard archaea, which is rRNA, not mRNA.

Confidence: moderate-high that nothing has been published. Absence from the main texts does not rule out something in supplements.

**Q3. RuBisCO-like protein and pilin/archaellin genes**

I found no reports. Targeted searches (web, and Europe PMC full text) for these genes together with intron or splicing terms returned nothing relevant. Confidence: moderate-high.

**Q4. Surveys from 2024–2026**

None I found looks for introns in protein-coding genes. The closest are:
- Goswami S, Ghosh AK, Sinha K. 2026. bioRxiv, doi:10.64898/2026.10.13.758758, not peer-reviewed. "Hidden introns and repeated gains shape the heavily interrupted tRNA genes of Asgard archaea." It covers 90 Asgard and 18 reference genomes and found that tRNAscan-SE "had missed 30% of intervening sequences". It deals with tRNA only.
- Mattick JSA, Malik S-B, Delwiche CF. 2024. bioRxiv, doi:10.1101/2024.12.10.627823. Group II introns (self-splicing elements that carry their own maturase gene) in Heimdallarchaeales, Lokiarchaeia, Thorarchaeia and Njordarchaeales. Asgard genomes "generally encode one or two copies". The evidence is computational only, and it does not say whether these sit inside host protein genes.
- The surveys of BHB motifs and circular RNAs are from 2012–2015, by Danan et al. and Berkemer et al. (Q1 items 4 and 5).
