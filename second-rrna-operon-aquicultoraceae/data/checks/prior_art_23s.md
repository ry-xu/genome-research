# Prior-art check: divergent / PTC-defective 23S paralog (Aquicultoraceae)
Prepared for Deneb, 2026-11-14. Abstracts came from the Europe PMC REST API (no email sent) unless a full-text page is named.
Scope: web search plus Europe PMC abstract and full-text queries. I did not read whole papers except where marked.

## Q1. Highly divergent second rRNA operons: known cases and largest divergences
Verified from the abstract text:
- VERIFIED. Wang, Zhang & Ramanan 1997, J Bacteriol 179:3270-3276, "The actinomycete Thermobispora bispora contains two distinct types of transcriptionally active 16S rRNA genes" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC179106/). The paper reports two 16S types that "differ at 98 nucleotide positions (6.4% of total nucleotides)". The genome has "three identical copies of the 23S RNA gene", so the 23S genes are NOT divergent. The authors add: "None of the base substitutions ... corresponds to any of the approximately 600 evolutionarily invariable or rarely variable nucleotides, indicating that both genes are functional."
- VERIFIED. Yap, Zhang & Wang 1999, J Bacteriol 181:5201-5209, "Distinct types of rRNA operons exist in the genome of the actinomycete Thermomonospora chromogena and evidence for horizontal transfer of an entire rRNA operon" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC94023/). Quote: "The coding sequences for the 16S and 23S rRNA genes differ by approximately 6 and 10%, respectively, between the two types of operons." The authors argue for "Normal functionality of rrnB" and for HGT from a T. bispora-like donor. This is the largest 23S divergence I found in bacteria (~90% identity).
- VERIFIED. Johansen et al. 2017, PLoS ONE 12:e0186393, "Highly divergent 16S rRNA sequences in ribosomal operons of Scytonema hyalinum (Cyanobacteria)" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5658200/). The 16S divergence is 7.3-9.0%. Quote: the 23S "was also found to be highly divergent from the gene in Type 2 operons (8.7%), and likewise had nearly identical secondary structure". The authors read this as functional and under selective constraint.
- VERIFIED. Dennis, Ziesche & Mylvaganam 1998, J Bacteriol, Haloarcula marismortui (archaeon) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC107503/). The two operons "differ at 74 of 1,472 positions, 39 of 2,922 positions, and 2 of 122 positions" for 16S, 23S and 5S. That puts the 23S at only ~1.3%. Lopez-Lopez et al. 2007, J Mol Evol 65:687-696 (doi 10.1007/s00239-007-9047-3) found operon B is expressed in a temperature-dependent way. Quote: "divergent rRNA genes can be adaptive, with different variants being functional under different environmental conditions".
- VERIFIED. Pei et al. 2010, Appl Environ Microbiol 76:3886-3897 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2893482/) surveyed 16S only, in 568 species. Quote: "from 0.06% to 20.38%; 0.55% +/- 1.46%". The extreme case "in B. afzelii (20.38%) appears to be a pseudogene", and the other divergent genes were "conserved" in secondary structure. Correction: Thermoanaerobacter tengcongensis is 6.70% in this paper. The "11.6%" figure did not come from text I read.
- VERIFIED: divergent operons as "specialized ribosomes". Neither case changes the PTC.
  - Song et al. 2019, Nat Microbiol 4:515-526 (doi 10.1038/s41564-018-0341-1). Quote: "ribosomes containing the most variable rRNAs, encoded by the rrnI operon ... direct the preferential translation of a subset of mRNAs in Vibrio vulnificus".
  - Kurylo et al. 2018, Cell Rep 25:236 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6312700/) describes E. coli rrsH, a 16S variant.
- VERIFIED: closest functional analogue, in a eukaryote and not yet peer-reviewed. Vignolini ... Baumgarten 2026, bioRxiv doi 10.64898/2026.06.17.732804, "A variant rRNA serves as a translational repressor in Plasmodium falciparum". The divergent LSU-only allele "appears to elongate inefficiently, likely due to divergences in the peptidyl transferase center", and it acts "as a dominant-negative repressor of translation". This is the only text I found that ties natural PTC divergence in an rRNA paralog to a phenotype. It is in a parasite, not a bacterium.
- Context only (abstract read, not about divergence): Brewer et al. 2020, ISME J 14:597-608 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6976660/) found unlinked 16S/23S genes are common, especially in Chloroflexi, Planctomycetes and Deinococcus-Thermus.
- Context only: Chodvadiya et al. 2025, bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.06.23.661116, built a 16S-23S-5S intragenomic-heterogeneity database of ~5000 genomes. Its abstract gives no maximum 23S divergence. NOT READ beyond the abstract.

NOT FOUND, within the searches below:
- Any report of rRNA operons, divergent or not, in Aquicultor, Aquicultoria or Aquicultoraceae. Europe PMC full text for "Aquicultor" AND (23S OR "rRNA operon") gave 0 hits. "Aquicultoria" AND rRNA gave 1 irrelevant review (cultivation strategies). LPSN lists only the 16S accession for Ca. Aquicultor secundus. I did not read Jiao et al. 2021 ISME J or Anantharaman et al. 2016 in full.
- Any natural bacterial 23S paralog reported as PTC-defective or non-functional. The exact phrases "divergent rRNA operon", "divergent rrn operon" and "divergent 23S rRNA gene" each gave 0 hits in Europe PMC. "rRNA pseudogene" AND bacteria gave nothing relevant. Every bacterial case above is ≤10% divergent in 23S and is argued to be functional, with conserved structure and conserved invariant sites.
- Taken together, a 23S that is ~72% identical (~28% divergent) to its own genome's 23S is ~3x the largest 23S divergence I found (T. chromogena, ~10%). It is also the only case I found that alters universally conserved PTC residues. This conclusion is limited to abstracts and the searches listed here.
- MAG caveat, my own inference rather than literature: in a single MAG, a divergent operon could be a binned contaminant contig. Synteny of the locus across two independent MAGs argues against that.

## Q2. Natural A2451 / G2251 / G2252 changes, and the two lab-mutant citations
- VERIFIED. Samaha RR, Green R, Noller HF 1995, Nature 377:309-314, "A base pair between tRNA and 23S rRNA in the peptidyl transferase centre of the ribosome" (PMID 7566085, abstract only). Quote: "A watson-Crick G-C pair between G2252 in a conserved hairpin loop of 23S rRNA and C74 at the acceptor end of tRNA is required for proper functional interaction of the CCA end of tRNA with the ribosomal P site." This paper covers G2252-C74 only.
- CORRECTION (VERIFIED). Kim DF & Green R 1999, Mol Cell 4:859-864, is titled "Base-pairing between 23S rRNA and tRNA in the ribosomal A site" (PMID 10619032, doi 10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80395-0, abstract only). It is about the A-loop, not G2251/G2252. Quote: "identified a base-pairing interaction between C75 of aminoacyl tRNA and G2553 of 23S rRNA."
- The correct lab-mutant citation for G2251 is Green R, Samaha RR, Noller HF 1997, J Mol Biol 266:40-50, "Mutations at nucleotides G2251 and U2585 of 23 S rRNA perturb the peptidyl transferase center of the ribosome" (PMID 9054969, abstract only). Quotes: "Mutations at either position confer a dominant lethal phenotype when the mutant 23 S rRNA is coexpressed with the endogenous wild-type 23 S rRNA" and "mutations at 2251 abolish both binding of the wild-type (CCA) tRNA fragment and peptidyl transferase activity". Note that tRNA mutations did NOT suppress the 2251 defects ("In neither case was the loss ... suppressed").
- For the G2251-C75 pair as an established fact, cite Feinberg JS & Joseph S 2006, J Mol Biol 364:1010-1020 (PMID 17045291, abstract only). Quote: "bases C74 and C75 of tRNA, form Watson-Crick base-pairs with G2252 and G2251, respectively, present in the conserved P-loop of 23 S rRNA." The same paper found the G2252-C74 pair "not essential for peptide bond formation" but important for peptide release.
- Related on A2451 (VERIFIED, abstract only): Polacek et al. 2001, Nature 411:498-501, "Ribosomal peptidyl transferase can withstand mutations at the putative catalytic nucleotide". Quote: "large ribosomal subunits with mutated A2451 showed significant peptidyl transferase activity". This means "A2451 changed implies dead" cannot rest on catalysis alone.
- NOT FOUND: any report of a NATURAL bacterial 23S lacking A2451 or with G2251/G2252 changed. I did not check organellar or mitochondrial LSU alignments.

## Q3. Mycoplasma hominis G2057A
- VERIFIED from PMC full text (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC128781/). Pereyre S, Gonzalez P, de Barbeyrac B, Darnige A, Renaudin H, Charron A, Raherison S, Bébéar C, Bébéar CM. "Mutations in 23S rRNA Account for Intrinsic Resistance to Macrolides in Mycoplasma hominis and Mycoplasma fermentans and for Acquired Resistance to Macrolides in M. hominis." Antimicrob Agents Chemother 2002;46(10):3142-3150, doi 10.1128/AAC.46.10.3142-3150.2002.
- Quotes from the text:
  - Abstract: "M. hominis harbored a G2057A transition in its 23S rRNA sequence".
  - Discussion: "The intrinsic erythromycin resistance was certainly linked to the G2057A transition".
  - Discussion, on M. pulmonis, M. hyopneumoniae and M. flocculare: they "harbored the same G2057A transition and were found to be intrinsically resistant to 14-membered macrolides".
- Caveat: the paper also names C2610U, which is natural in M. hominis. Quote: "the G2057A and the C2610U transitions in M. hominis ribosomes were probably the main mechanism of intrinsic drug resistance." Cite both changes, not G2057A alone.

## Q4. Is a divergent rRNA copy ever shown to be NOT expressed? (Deneb, 14 Nov evening; web search only)
Sources are search-result snippets plus the Q1 entries above. I have not read the papers in full, so treat everything here as unverified unless Q1 already verified it.
- Every divergent-operon case in Q1 was reported as expressed:
  - T. bispora: "two distinct types of transcriptionally active 16S rRNA genes" (title, verified above).
  - T. chromogena rrnB: the abstract cites "detection of correctly processed rRNAs in the cell". Snippet only.
  - Haloarcula operon B: expressed, temperature-dependent (verified above).
- Streptomyces coelicolor: one snippet says primer extension showed "all of the heterogeneous rRNA molecules are expressed and assembled into ribosomes". I don't know which paper; NOT VERIFIED.
- A UFO1 16S copy with an insert: the insert was absent from cDNA, read as excised from the rRNA rather than as a silent copy. Snippet only; NOT VERIFIED.
- NOT FOUND in 3 web searches: any bacterial rRNA copy shown by RNA reads or RNA hybridisation to be transcriptionally silent while present in DNA. No Aquicultoraceae metatranscriptome analysis either.
- The 2015 Crystal Geyser time series is Probst et al. 2017 Nat Microbiol (metagenomes). I did not find which paper the 2015-05 metatranscriptomes (PRJNA622041-4) belong to.
